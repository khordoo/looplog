This document is the content handoff for the catalog described in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md); it is not medical advice and it does not replace an in-person assessment. “High fit” means the linked demonstration and the written setup agree closely. “Medium fit” means the movement is clear and the source is credible, but the video’s band length, camera angle, or exact variant needs a final visual check before production. ## Equipment convention and shared safety copy

The available bands are 41-inch-style continuous loop bands: purple #1 (5–35 lb nominal), red #2 (10–50 lb), blue #3 (25–80 lb), and green #4 (50–120 lb). Nominal ranges vary with stretch, stance, grip, and band combination; they are not fixed weights. Use a stable, nonslip floor and inspect the band for cuts, thinning, cracks, or sticky spots before every session. Keep fingers clear of pinches, never wrap a band around the neck, and stop if pain is sharp, radiating, or worsening. A band that slips can recoil; release tension deliberately and never step off a loaded band.

For all band movements, start with the lightest band that permits the full listed range without compensating. “Shorten” means take more band between the hands or feet to increase prestretch; do not knot the band. When a 41-inch loop is awkward for a new user, use the bodyweight or incline regression until a safe, repeatable setup is available.

## Catalog definitions

### `lower-front-squat-band` — Band front squat

- **Category:** Squat; primary quadriceps and gluteus maximus; secondary adductors, hamstrings, and trunk bracing.
- **Equipment/setup:** One continuous 41-inch loop. Stand on its midpoint with feet about shoulder width and toes slightly out. Bring the other half up the front of the body and rest it across the upper shoulders/traps, not the throat; cross the forearms to pin it. Use a light band or shorten only enough to create safe starting tension.
- **Execution:** Inhale and brace; sit hips down and slightly back while knees track over the middle toes. Descend only as far as the feet stay flat and the spine stays neutral. Exhale while driving through the whole foot to stand tall without snapping the knees.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale on the 2–3-second descent; brief controlled pause if comfortable; exhale on the 1–2-second ascent. No breath-holding for a beginner.
- **Form cues:** “Tripod foot”; “knees follow toes”; “ribs stacked over pelvis”; “band stays centered on shoulders”; “stand by pushing the floor away.”
- **Common mistakes:** Band on the neck; heels lifting; knees collapsing inward; dropping quickly; folding the chest or overextending at lockout.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: bodyweight squat to a chair or use a lighter band. Harder: wider stance only if comfortable, shorten the band, add a second band, or use a 3-second eccentric.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `lower-reverse-lunge`, `lower-split-squat` (unilateral squat); chair squat within the same squat category.
- **Safety warning:** A loop across the shoulders can slip upward; stop and reset if it approaches the neck. Do not use a heavy band if you cannot safely disengage it at the top.
- **Demonstration:** [BarBend — How to: Resistance Band Front Squat](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5K-KlUlPo40) (long loop under feet and across shoulders, no anchor). **Confidence: high / fit high.** BarBend’s linked exercise is the same under-foot, shoulder-held movement; recheck the current video frame before embedding.

### `upper-row-seated-feet` — Seated band row around the feet

- **Category:** Horizontal pull; primary lats, rhomboids, middle trapezius; secondary biceps and rear deltoids.
- **Equipment/setup:** Sit on the floor with legs extended or softly bent. Loop the 41-inch band around both feet at mid-foot, leaving the loop behind the feet; hold both sides with a neutral grip. Sit tall enough that the band has mild tension without pulling the feet toward the face. For C4 staggered variant, stand in a split stance on the band and row both sides while maintaining a small hip hinge.
- **Execution:** Brace, keep the chest open, and draw elbows toward the ribs until hands reach the lower ribs. Pause with shoulder blades moving back and down, then reach arms forward slowly without rounding or losing foot contact.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Exhale during the 1–2-second pull; inhale during a 2–3-second return. Keep a brief pause at the end of each row.
- **Form cues:** “Neck long”; “elbows skim the ribs”; “move shoulder blades, not the head”; “feet stay planted”; “no rocking.”
- **Common mistakes:** Rounding the lumbar spine; leaning backward to create momentum; shrugging; pulling elbows far behind the body; letting the band go slack.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: bend knees more, use a lighter band, or perform one-arm bodyweight scapular rows with a towel. Harder: extend the legs, shorten the band, add a pause, or use the C4 staggered stance.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `upper-row-bentover-band`, `upper-pull-apart-band` (horizontal/scapular pull).
- **Safety warning:** Check that the loop is centered on both feet and cannot roll over the toes. If it slips, lower the band under control; do not jerk backward.
- **Demonstration:** [Lifetime Lean — Resistance Band Seated Row](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SgmTwc5keKk) (band wrapped around feet, neutral spine, no anchor). **Confidence: high / fit high.** The description explicitly matches the default A2 setup; the C4 staggered option is a documented in-app variant, not the linked demo.

### `lower-rdl-band` — Band Romanian deadlift

- **Category:** Hinge; primary hamstrings and gluteus maximus; secondary adductors, erectors, lats, and grip.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand on the loop’s midpoint with feet hip width. Hold the loop’s opposite half in both hands in front of the thighs; if extra length remains, grip lower or take a small second fold, never a knot. Start with soft knees, long spine, and enough tension that the band is not slack.
- **Execution:** Inhale and brace; push hips back while the shins stay nearly vertical and hands stay close to the legs. Lower until hamstrings are loaded without lumbar rounding. Exhale and drive hips forward to stand tall; finish with glutes, not a back lean.
- **Breathing/tempo:** 3-second lower, 1-second pause at the hamstring limit, 1–2-second rise. Exhale through the rise.
- **Form cues:** “Hips back”; “band close to legs”; “soft knees”; “sternum and pelvis move together”; “finish tall, not leaned back.”
- **Common mistakes:** Squatting instead of hinging; rounding the back; letting hands drift forward; locking knees; bouncing from the bottom.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: hip-hinge drill with a wall or a lighter band. Harder: shorten grip, add a 2-second bottom pause, or use a staggered stance only after the supported single-leg hinge is controlled.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `lower-good-morning-band`, `lower-single-leg-rdl-supported` (hinge).
- **Safety warning:** Heavy bands can pull the shoulders forward abruptly. Do not chase floor depth; end the descent when neutral spine or foot pressure changes.
- **Demonstration:** [Band Bar Studios — Resistance Band RDL tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPXnuQjOz0E) (long loop held under feet and in hands, no door anchor). **Confidence: high / fit high.** The video description specifically identifies a long loop and RDL setup; it is a commercial coaching channel, so recheck the visible band length and grip.

### `upper-pushup-band` — Band-resisted push-up (incline regression)

- **Category:** Horizontal push; primary chest, triceps, and anterior deltoid; secondary serratus anterior and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** For the banded version, place the loop across the upper back (not the neck), pass each side under the palms, and set hands just outside the shoulders. Use a band light enough to maintain a straight plank. No anchor. For the A4/C2 regression, place hands on a sturdy counter/bench or use knees; bodyweight incline is preferred before adding a band.
- **Execution:** Screw hands into the floor, brace glutes and ribs, lower chest between hands with elbows about 30–45° from the body, then press the floor away until elbows extend without locking hard. Keep the band centered as the torso moves as one unit.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale on a 2–3-second lower; exhale on the press. Pause briefly at the bottom only if alignment is stable.
- **Form cues:** “Band across shoulder blades”; “hands under shoulders”; “ribs and hips rise together”; “elbows track, not flare”; “push the floor away.”
- **Common mistakes:** Band around the neck; hips sagging or piking; hands too wide; elbows flaring; holding breath; band under only one palm.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: wall/incline, knees, or bodyweight floor push-up. Harder: lower the incline, use a stronger/shortened band, or add a controlled pause; never stack bands until a single band is secure.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `upper-floor-press-band`, incline push-up, or floor push-up (horizontal push).
- **Safety warning:** A loop under the palms can shoot backward if it slips. Use a dry floor and stop if the band rolls toward the neck or wrists. Band-resisted push-ups are not the first exposure for a novice.
- **Demonstration:** [James Grage / Undersun — Chest & Triceps with Dumbbells and Bands](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vFTIpxW_SMY&t=29s) (banded push-up segment at 0:29, loop across back and under hands, no door anchor). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Verified creator and the time-stamped segment match the working variant; the app should still show the bodyweight/incline regression in writing.

### `upper-press-overhead-band` — Standing band overhead press

- **Category:** Vertical push; primary deltoids and triceps; secondary upper chest, serratus, and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand on the middle of a 41-inch loop with one or both feet, feet about hip/shoulder width. Hold the opposite half in both hands at shoulder height, palms forward or neutral, with the band behind the forearms and not across the throat. A staggered stance may improve balance; no anchor.
- **Execution:** Brace with ribs stacked, press hands vertically while keeping wrists over elbows, and finish with arms overhead without shrugging or arching. Lower slowly to shoulder height while keeping some tension.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale at the start; exhale through the 1–2-second press; inhale during a 2–3-second lower.
- **Form cues:** “Feet own the band”; “elbows slightly forward”; “ribs down”; “press in a straight path”; “neck stays long.”
- **Common mistakes:** Band behind the neck; excessive backbend; elbows flaring; pressing through painful range; starting with slack and snapping into tension.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: one-arm press with a lighter band, seated press, or no band. Harder: shorten grip, use a staggered stance, add a pause overhead, or use a second band only with a secure release plan.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `upper-floor-press-band`, incline push-up (push category).
- **Safety warning:** Do not press a band that cannot be brought to shoulder height and released safely. Keep the loop away from the face and neck; shoulder pain is a stop signal.
- **Demonstration:** [Undersun Fitness — Full Body Resistance Band Training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nT3G-5f74k&t=397s) (shoulder press segment at 6:37, long loops held under feet, no anchor). **Confidence: medium-high / fit medium-high.** Verified manufacturer/coaching source and matching unanchored press; it is a follow-along segment, so confirm its current timestamp and visible grip before production.

### `core-dead-bug` — Dead bug

- **Category:** Anti-extension core; primary deep abdominals and rectus abdominis; secondary hip flexors and shoulder stabilizers.
- **Equipment/setup:** Lie on your back with hips and knees at 90°, arms toward the ceiling, pelvis neutral or gently posteriorly tilted. No band is required; a very light loop around the feet is an optional progression only after the bodyweight version is controlled.
- **Execution:** Brace as if preparing for a gentle tap. Slowly lower the opposite arm and leg toward the floor without letting the back arch; return and alternate. Use a shorter reach or heel tap if the low back lifts.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Exhale during each reach; inhale on return. Use a 3-second reach and 1-second reset.
- **Form cues:** “Back stays heavy”; “ribs down”; “move opposite limbs”; “small range is valid”; “quiet pelvis.”
- **Common mistakes:** Arching the lumbar spine; moving too quickly; reaching both sides too far; shrugging; forcing a range that causes hip or back pain.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: heel taps, one limb at a time, or arms still. Harder: longer lever, slower eccentric, or a light loop at the feet with no loss of control.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `core-bird-dog`, `core-side-plank` (core control).
- **Safety warning:** Do not add a band around the feet until a bodyweight dead bug is pain-free and the pelvis remains quiet. Stop for back pain, abdominal doming/coning, or breath loss.
- **Demonstration:** [Hinge Health — Dead Bug, guide from physical therapists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GbSC02oU3To) (bodyweight dead bug, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Clinical source and exact default setup; the optional band progression is intentionally not part of the video.

### `lower-reverse-lunge` — Reverse lunge (initially bodyweight)

- **Category:** Unilateral squat; primary quadriceps, gluteus maximus, and adductors; secondary hamstrings, calves, and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand near a wall or chair for optional fingertip support, feet hip width, no band initially. Later, a loop can be placed under the front foot and held at the shoulders only if the bodyweight pattern is stable; this is not the V1 starting setup.
- **Execution:** Step one foot straight back, land softly on the ball of that foot, bend both knees under control, and lower until the front thigh is comfortable and the rear knee approaches (not crashes into) the floor. Drive through the front whole foot to return.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale stepping/lowering for 2–3 seconds; exhale as you push up. Pause briefly to regain balance before alternating.
- **Form cues:** “Step back like a train track”; “front foot stays flat”; “knee follows toes”; “torso tall”; “quiet, controlled return.”
- **Common mistakes:** Narrow tightrope stance; front heel lifting; knee collapsing inward; dropping onto rear knee; pushing off the rear toes; rushing the step.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: hold support, reduce depth, or perform a step-back toe tap. Harder: full range, slower eccentric, then light band or load once stable.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `lower-split-squat`, `lower-front-squat-band` (unilateral/bilateral squat).
- **Safety warning:** Start bodyweight as [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) specifies. Use a stable support for balance; do not add the band while the user is still reaching for the floor or losing alignment.
- **Demonstration:** [Hinge Health — Reverse Lunge, guide from physical therapists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6rKA_et3dGk) (bodyweight step-back lunge, no anchor). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Clinical source and exact initial setup; a future band progression needs its own visual review.

### `upper-floor-press-band` — Band floor press

- **Category:** Horizontal push; primary chest and triceps; secondary anterior deltoid and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** Lie on the floor with knees bent and feet planted. Pass the 41-inch loop across the upper back, under the shoulder blades, and hold the two sides in the hands; the loop should not pass near the neck. Keep elbows on the floor or just above it, no anchor.
- **Execution:** Set shoulder blades gently down, wrists stacked over elbows, and press hands toward the ceiling until arms are nearly straight. Lower until upper arms lightly touch the floor while maintaining control and band symmetry.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale on a 2–3-second lower; exhale through the press. Pause for one count at the floor if shoulders stay comfortable.
- **Form cues:** “Band under shoulder blades”; “wrists over elbows”; “feet stay planted”; “elbows about 30–45°”; “press up, not forward.”
- **Common mistakes:** Band across the neck; elbows flaring; wrists bending back; feet sliding; bouncing elbows; using a band too strong to set up safely.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: bodyweight floor press is not meaningful, so use incline push-up or a lighter band. Harder: shorten grip, press one side at a time with careful symmetry, or add a pause; avoid heavy bands without a spotter-like release plan.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `upper-pushup-band`, `upper-press-overhead-band` (push category).
- **Safety warning:** Set up and release the band while lying with elbows bent, never from a stretched overhead position. Stop if the band migrates toward the neck or shoulder pain appears.
- **Demonstration:** [Fitway — Resistance Band Floor Press](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h1uU13KkCXw) (band under upper back, hands pressing upward, no door anchor). **Confidence: medium / fit high.** The written video description exactly matches the setup; app maintainer should confirm the current video still shows a continuous loop rather than a handled band.

### `lower-good-morning-band` — Band good morning

- **Category:** Hinge; primary hamstrings, gluteus maximus, and erectors; secondary adductors and upper back.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand on the loop with feet about hip width. Bring the other half behind the upper shoulders/traps, holding it lightly at the chest/shoulders; keep it away from the neck. Use a very light band at first, no anchor.
- **Execution:** Brace, unlock knees, and push hips back while the band gently resists the torso. Hinge only until hamstrings are loaded and the spine remains neutral; drive hips forward to stand tall.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale and brace before the hinge; lower for 2–3 seconds; exhale as the hips extend. No bouncing.
- **Form cues:** “Hips travel back”; “band on traps, not neck”; “knees soft”; “spine long”; “squeeze glutes to finish.”
- **Common mistakes:** Squatting down; rounding the back; band at the cervical spine; knees collapsing; standing by arching the lumbar spine.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: bodyweight hip hinge or band RDL. Harder: shorten the band, add a pause, or use a slightly wider stance if it preserves alignment.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `lower-rdl-band`, `lower-single-leg-rdl-supported` (hinge).
- **Safety warning:** Good-morning bands can pull the torso forward and can recoil upward if they slip from the shoulders. Do not use the heaviest green band for a novice; stop if the band approaches the neck.
- **Demonstration:** [Banded Good Mornings — Exercise Demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7PKCrUmE0NE) (continuous loop, feet-to-shoulders, no door anchor; description specifically references a 41-inch loop). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Exact equipment and movement match; inspect the current frame for safe shoulder placement when embedding.

### `upper-row-bentover-band` — Bent-over band row

- **Category:** Horizontal pull; primary lats, rhomboids, and middle trapezius; secondary biceps, rear deltoids, erectors, and grip.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand on the middle of a 41-inch loop with feet hip width and hold the other half in both hands. Hinge to roughly 30–45° with a neutral back, soft knees, and band taut; no anchor.
- **Execution:** Brace and pull elbows toward the hips/ribs, keeping the torso angle steady. Pause at the top without shrugging; lower hands until arms are long while maintaining tension.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Exhale on the 1–2-second row; inhale on a 2–3-second return. Keep the hinge isometric.
- **Form cues:** “Chest long”; “hips back”; “elbows to pockets”; “neck follows spine”; “no torso swing.”
- **Common mistakes:** Standing upright during the pull; rounding the low back; shrugging; yanking; band losing tension; knees locked.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: higher torso angle or seated row. Harder: shorten grip, add a pause, or use a staggered stance only when stable.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `upper-row-seated-feet`, `upper-pull-apart-band` (pull).
- **Safety warning:** Keep the loop centered under both feet and check it is not caught on a toe. Stop if the hinge becomes a low-back endurance test rather than a controlled row.
- **Demonstration:** [Fitinsane — Resistance Band Bent-Over Row](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qid9sie2XLs) (band under feet, hinged torso, both hands, no anchor). **Confidence: medium-high / fit high.** The linked coaching article identifies the video and matching setup; verify the current upload remains accessible.

### `upper-pull-apart-band` — Band pull-apart

- **Category:** Scapular/postural pull; primary rear deltoids, rhomboids, and middle trapezius; secondary rotator cuff and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** Hold a light 41-inch loop in both hands at shoulder height with arms straight or softly bent. Use enough width that the loop begins taut; no anchor and no wrapping around the body.
- **Execution:** Keep ribs stacked and pull hands laterally until the band approaches the chest, moving from the shoulders. Pause, then return slowly without letting the band snap.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Exhale while opening; inhale during the 2–3-second return. Use 8–15 easy, crisp reps rather than fatigue grinding.
- **Form cues:** “Shoulders away from ears”; “ribs down”; “hands move sideways”; “squeeze between shoulder blades”; “return quietly.”
- **Common mistakes:** Shrugging; arching the back; bending elbows to row; pulling behind the body; using a band too heavy to control.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: widen grip or use a lighter band. Harder: narrow grip, add a 2-second hold, or use a staggered stance for trunk control.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `upper-row-seated-feet`, bodyweight shoulder-blade retraction (pull/postural category).
- **Safety warning:** Use a light band; heavy 41-inch bands can irritate shoulders and can recoil toward the face. Keep hands out of the loop if the band is not fully controlled.
- **Demonstration:** [FIT.nl — Resistance Band Pull Apart](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WqdNDTTe-9g) (standing pull-apart at shoulder height, no anchor). **Confidence: medium-high / fit high.** Coaching source and direct movement match; low view count is not a quality claim, so manually inspect the current video before shipping.

### `core-side-plank` — Side plank

- **Category:** Anti-lateral-flexion core; primary obliques and quadratus lumborum; secondary gluteus medius, shoulder stabilizers, and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** Lie on one side on a mat. Place elbow under shoulder, forearm forward, legs stacked; start from knees if needed. No band. Keep head, ribs, pelvis, and legs in one line.
- **Execution:** Brace and lift hips until the body forms a straight line; hold without rolling forward or backward. Lower with control and switch sides. The knee-supported version keeps knees bent with hips extended.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Slow nasal inhale and controlled exhale during a 10–30-second hold; do not hold breath.
- **Form cues:** “Elbow under shoulder”; “push floor away”; “hips stay stacked”; “ribs over pelvis”; “neck long.”
- **Common mistakes:** Shoulder collapsing; hips sagging; pelvis rotating; holding breath; using a duration that causes shaking and loss of line.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: knees bent, shorter hold, or side-lying brace. Harder: full feet stack, longer hold, or top-leg lift; do not add a band in V1.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `core-dead-bug`, `core-bird-dog` (core control).
- **Safety warning:** Stop if the bottom shoulder or neck bears sharp pain. Do not chase a long hold after alignment is lost; reduce lever or use knees.
- **Demonstration:** [Hinge Health — Side Plank, guide from physical therapists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qbuqsJ9CuQA) (knee/full side-plank options, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Clinical source and exact bodyweight movement.

### `lower-split-squat` — Split squat

- **Category:** Unilateral squat; primary quadriceps and gluteus maximus; secondary adductors, hamstrings, calves, and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand in a stable split stance with front foot flat and back heel lifted. Start bodyweight or use a wall/chair for support. Optional band progression: place one end under the front foot and bring the other half around the upper shoulders/arms, never the neck; no anchor.
- **Execution:** Lower straight down by bending both knees while keeping the front knee tracking over the middle toes and pelvis square. Stop before the rear knee crashes; drive through the front whole foot to rise.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale on a 2–3-second descent; exhale through the ascent. Pause briefly at the bottom if balanced.
- **Form cues:** “Long train tracks”; “front heel heavy”; “hips descend, not drift”; “knee follows toes”; “torso quiet.”
- **Common mistakes:** Narrow stance; front heel lifting; knee caving; pushing off rear foot; band rubbing the neck; bouncing.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: assisted or reduced depth, then bodyweight. Harder: slower eccentric, increase depth, then light loop band or add a pause.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `lower-reverse-lunge`, `lower-front-squat-band` (squat category).
- **Safety warning:** Learn the bodyweight version first. A band around the shoulders can slip during a single-leg movement; use a light band and a clear release path.
- **Demonstration:** [TB12 Sports — Resistance Band Split Squats](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YxyVk-vRtX8) (long loop under lead foot and around the upper arms, no anchor). **Confidence: high / fit high.** TB12 Sports Therapy source and long-loop setup match; the app should cue the bodyweight/support regression before this loaded version.

### `lower-single-leg-rdl-supported` — Supported single-leg Romanian deadlift

- **Category:** Unilateral hinge; primary hamstring and glute of the stance leg; secondary hip stabilizers, foot/ankle, erectors, and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand beside a wall, countertop, or sturdy chair and use one hand for light support. Place weight through the whole stance foot, soften its knee, and let the other foot hover or lightly toe-tap behind. Start bodyweight; a light loop held under the stance foot and in the opposite hand is an optional later progression, not required for C3.
- **Execution:** Hinge at the stance hip while the free leg reaches back, keeping pelvis square and back long. Lower only as far as balance and neutral spine allow; drive through the stance foot to return. Use fingertip support rather than pulling with the arm.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale/brace into a 3-second hinge; exhale through the 1–2-second return. Pause at the deepest controlled point.
- **Form cues:** “Soft stance knee”; “hips stay square”; “whole foot grounded”; “support hand is light”; “reach long, not high.”
- **Common mistakes:** Locking the stance knee; rotating open; reaching the free leg too high; collapsing the arch; touching down heavily; using the support arm to haul upright.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: kickstand/staggered RDL or two-hand wall support. Harder: less hand support, longer pause, then light band in the opposite hand.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `lower-rdl-band`, `lower-good-morning-band` (hinge).
- **Safety warning:** Balance is the limiting factor, not band strength. Keep support within reach and do not add a band until the bodyweight version is controlled on both sides.
- **Demonstration:** [Robertson Training Systems — How to Single-Leg RDL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pAOEdLYHQPk) (single-leg hinge coaching; use the article’s supported/kickstand regression first). **Confidence: medium / fit medium.** Reputable coaching source and exact hinge, but the linked video is primarily unassisted and may show a load; treat this as a form reference and manually confirm the support regression before embedding.

### `upper-curl-band` — Band biceps curl

- **Category:** Elbow flexion; primary biceps brachii; secondary brachialis, brachioradialis, and grip.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand on the loop’s midpoint with feet hip width (wider feet increase tension). Hold the opposite half in both hands, palms forward, arms at sides, elbows near ribs. No anchor or handles.
- **Execution:** Brace, keep upper arms still, and curl hands toward shoulders without rolling wrists or leaning back. Squeeze briefly, then lower under control until elbows are nearly straight while retaining mild tension.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Exhale on the 1–2-second curl; inhale on a 2–3-second lower. No swinging.
- **Form cues:** “Elbows pinned”; “wrists neutral”; “shoulders quiet”; “curl, don’t heave”; “slow lower.”
- **Common mistakes:** Leaning backward; elbows drifting forward; snapping elbows straight; band slack at the bottom; grip twisting; feet too close for safe tension.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: lighter band, staggered stance, or one arm at a time. Harder: wider stance, shorten grip, add a 2-second squeeze, or use a slow eccentric.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `upper-row-seated-feet` (pull), hammer-grip curl with the same band, or bodyweight isometric curl against the opposite hand.
- **Safety warning:** Do not stand on a band that is visibly damaged or too short to reach the hands without jerking. Keep band tension low enough to release by lowering the hands, not by stepping off.
- **Demonstration:** [Undersun Fitness — Single Arm Biceps Curls with Resistance Bands](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GgfPjyfR0t0) (continuous band held/loaded from the floor, no door anchor). **Confidence: medium-high / fit medium-high.** Verified manufacturer/coaching source; the clip is single-arm, while V1 uses two hands, so manually confirm the loop is used underfoot and not attached before embedding.

### `lower-calf-raise` — Standing calf raise

- **Category:** Plantarflexion; primary gastrocnemius and soleus; secondary foot/ankle stabilizers.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand near a wall or chair for balance with feet hip width and toes pointing forward. Bodyweight is the V1 load; a loop over the shoulders or held under the forefoot is not needed and should not be added until an explicit catalog revision.
- **Execution:** Press the floor away through the base of the big toe and second toe, rise as high as balance permits, pause, and lower slowly until heels touch. Keep knees softly unlocked and weight even.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Exhale on the 1–2-second rise; inhale on a 2–3-second lower. Pause at the top without bouncing.
- **Form cues:** “Tripod foot”; “rise straight up”; “heels track behind toes”; “quiet support hand”; “slow lower.”
- **Common mistakes:** Rolling to the outer foot; bending knees into a squat; bouncing; dropping heels; using unstable furniture; rushing because the movement feels easy.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: hold two hands on support or use a smaller range. Harder: single-leg supported raise, longer pause, or a step only if ankle range is pain-free.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `lower-front-squat-band`, seated heel raise, or supported single-leg heel raise (calf category).
- **Safety warning:** Calf/Achilles pain is a stop signal, especially after inactivity. Keep a stable support close; do not use a heavy loop on the feet for V1.
- **Demonstration:** [Coury & Buehler Physical Therapy — Standing Heel Raises](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MW2WG5l-fYE) (bodyweight standing heel raise with support, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Direct physical-therapy demonstration matches the V1 bodyweight setup.

### `core-bird-dog` — Bird dog

- **Category:** Anti-rotation/anti-extension core; primary trunk stabilizers and multifidi; secondary glutes, shoulders, and hamstrings.
- **Equipment/setup:** Begin on hands and knees with hands beneath shoulders and knees beneath hips. Use a mat. No band in V1; a band around opposite hand/foot is a future progression only.
- **Execution:** Brace and extend one arm and the opposite leg until both are long without arching or rotating. Hold briefly, return hand and knee quietly, and alternate sides.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Exhale into the reach, inhale on return; use a 2–3-second reach and 1–2-second hold.
- **Form cues:** “Tabletop stays still”; “reach long”; “hips square”; “toes point down”; “quiet return.”
- **Common mistakes:** Arching the low back; opening the hip; shrugging; lifting too high; shifting weight before reaching; moving too fast.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: arm-only, leg-only, or toe/hand taps. Harder: longer holds, slow returns, then a light band around opposite hand/foot after bodyweight control.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `core-dead-bug`, `core-side-plank` (core control).
- **Safety warning:** Do not add a band while balance is poor; the band can pull the pelvis into rotation. Keep the neck neutral and stop for wrist, shoulder, or back pain.
- **Demonstration:** [Margaret Martin PT — Bird Dog Exercise for Beginners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LaLKNS7mxrk) (quadruped opposite arm/leg, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Physical-therapist source and exact default setup.

## Optional five-minute desk reset

These movements are deliberately not logged as workout progression. Use a comfortable range, one round, and stop for pain, dizziness, numbness, or loss of balance. Each item below has exactly one demonstration link.

### `reset-march` — Marching in place

- **Category:** Low-impact warm-up and circulation.
- **Equipment/setup:** Clear floor; stand near a counter if balance is uncertain. No band.
- **Execution:** Alternate lifting feet a few inches, swing arms naturally, and land softly. Keep the torso tall and make the range smaller if the hips or knees feel stiff.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Easy conversational breathing for 45–60 seconds; gradually increase cadence without impact.
- **Form cues:** “Tall posture”; “soft landing”; “opposite arm/leg”; “eyes forward”; “quiet feet.”
- **Common mistakes:** Holding breath; leaning backward; stomping; lifting knees too high; marching close to furniture.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: toe taps or seated march. Harder: slightly faster cadence or gentle arm reach; no band needed.
- **Compatible alternatives:** Easy walk, seated march, or step-back toe tap.
- **Safety warning:** Keep a hand near stable support and avoid rapid high knees if balance is limited.
- **Demonstration:** [Margaret Martin PT — Marching in Place](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e_uIJdhwnj4) (standing march, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Physical-therapist warm-up demonstration matches exactly.

### `reset-thoracic-rotation` — Thoracic rotation / book opener

- **Category:** Thoracic mobility; secondary shoulder and chest mobility.
- **Equipment/setup:** Lie on one side on a mat, hips and knees comfortably bent/stacked, arms straight in front with palms together. No band.
- **Execution:** Keep knees and pelvis stacked while sweeping the top arm open and rotating the upper back until the chest faces upward as far as comfortable. Return slowly and switch sides.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale as the chest opens; exhale at the comfortable end range. Use 5–8 slow reps per side.
- **Form cues:** “Knees stay stacked”; “rotation from mid-back”; “shoulder blade relaxes”; “no forcing”; “breathe into ribs.”
- **Common mistakes:** Letting knees separate; twisting from the low back; pulling the arm with momentum; forcing the shoulder to the floor.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: smaller arm sweep or seated rotation with arms crossed. Harder: longer exhale at end range; no added band.
- **Compatible alternatives:** Quadruped thoracic rotation or seated gentle rotation.
- **Safety warning:** Keep rotation pain-free; people with known spinal restrictions should follow clinician guidance and avoid forcing end range.
- **Demonstration:** [Physio REHAB — Thoracic Rotation (Book Openers)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uzpyRbwYgGA) (side-lying book opener, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Verified physiotherapy channel and exact setup.

### `reset-hip-flexor-stretch` — Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch

- **Category:** Hip-flexor mobility; secondary quadriceps and ankle mobility.
- **Equipment/setup:** Half-kneel with one knee on a mat and the opposite foot in front. Use a chair or wall for balance. Keep pelvis square; no band.
- **Execution:** Gently tuck the tailbone/ribs, squeeze the rear glute, and shift the whole pelvis forward a few centimeters until a mild stretch is felt at the front of the rear hip. Do not lunge deeply or arch the back. Switch sides.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Breathe slowly for 20–30 seconds per side; no bouncing or forced end range.
- **Form cues:** “Rear glute on”; “tailbone down”; “ribs stacked”; “front foot planted”; “small shift.”
- **Common mistakes:** Anteriorly tilting the pelvis; arching low back; pushing knee past pain; collapsing front foot; holding breath.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: standing split-stance stretch with support. Harder: add a gentle rear-foot quad hold only if knee is comfortable; no band.
- **Compatible alternatives:** Standing hip-flexor stretch or couch-supported half-kneel.
- **Safety warning:** Stretching should feel mild, not pinching in the front of the hip or knee. Use padding and support; stop for numbness or sharp pain.
- **Demonstration:** [Hart Osteopathy — Hip Flexor Stretch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UW1RAPG2-Yc) (half-kneeling pelvic-control cues, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Osteopathy source and exact mobility goal; the linked “updated” video ID was extracted from the clinic’s embedded player.

### `reset-scapular-setting` — Shoulder-blade retractions

- **Category:** Postural control; primary middle/lower trapezius and rhomboids; no resistance.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand or sit tall with arms relaxed by the sides. A wall or mirror can provide feedback; no band and no door anchor.
- **Execution:** Without shrugging, gently draw shoulder blades back and slightly down, hold 2–3 seconds, then let them return to neutral. Keep the neck and ribs relaxed; this is a small movement, not a chest thrust.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Normal breathing; 6–10 slow repetitions or 30–45 seconds.
- **Form cues:** “Neck long”; “shoulders away from ears”; “small glide”; “ribs quiet”; “release fully.”
- **Common mistakes:** Pinching hard; shrugging; arching the back; thrusting the chin; holding the shoulder blades down continuously rather than moving them.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: seated with back support. Harder: add a 5-second hold or perform wall-supported scapular setting; no band for the desk reset.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `upper-pull-apart-band` (loaded pull-apart) or gentle wall angel.
- **Safety warning:** Do not force the shoulders “back and down” if that creates pain, tingling, or a pinching sensation. The goal is control, not maximal squeeze.
- **Demonstration:** [Cornell Health PT — Scapular Setting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sPSOQ7RwpgQ) (unloaded scapular setting, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** University physical-therapy library link and exact bodyweight desk-reset movement.

### `reset-bodyweight-squat` — Bodyweight squat

- **Category:** Movement break; primary quadriceps and glutes; secondary hamstrings, adductors, calves, and trunk.
- **Equipment/setup:** Stand with feet just outside hip width, toes slightly out, near a chair/counter for optional support. No band.
- **Execution:** Reach hips back and down as if sitting, keep feet planted and knees tracking over toes, then stand by pushing through the whole foot. Use a chair target and a smaller range if needed.
- **Breathing/tempo:** Inhale down for 2 seconds; exhale up for 1–2 seconds; 6–10 easy reps.
- **Form cues:** “Sit between the feet”; “whole foot down”; “knees follow toes”; “chest and pelvis move together”; “stand tall.”
- **Common mistakes:** Heels lifting; knees collapsing; dropping onto the chair; rounding at the bottom; locking knees aggressively.
- **Easier/harder:** Easier: higher chair, partial range, or sit-to-stand. Harder: slower lower or a 2-second pause; do not add a band in the desk reset.
- **Compatible alternatives:** `lower-front-squat-band` when it is a scheduled strength movement, or sit-to-stand.
- **Safety warning:** Keep the chair/counter stable and leave enough space behind. This reset should feel restorative; do not turn it into a fatigue set.
- **Demonstration:** [Keep It Moving Physical Therapy & Wellness — Bodyweight Squat](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r0kHBKP02y0) (unloaded squat, no equipment). **Confidence: high / fit high.** Direct physical-therapy source and exact desk-reset variant.
